## Information gain
The reframe: agencies treat migration as a dreaded chore; it is actually (a) a client-acquisition
wedge and (b) a repeatable service line.
1. Two shapes of migration: onboarding one client (free, part of winning them) vs bulk-moving a whole
   portfolio onto one managed account (a project you can charge for or absorb to cut your own costs).
2. Migration as a WEDGE: "we'll move you for free" removes the biggest objection to switching, which is
   the pain of moving. Offering to do it is often what wins the client.
3. Running it at scale: a WAVE, not twenty panics. Batch similar sites, staging-first, verify to a
   definition of done, DNS last, one wave at a time. Links zero-downtime for the per-site mechanics.
4. De-risking: free migration assistance + staging + a pre-cutover backup means the client's live site
   never goes dark; if the new copy fails verification, you simply don't cut over.
5. Client comms: the reassurance the client actually needs ("your current site keeps running until we
   flip the switch, and we flip it only when the new one is proven").
6. Pricing: onboarding migration free as acquisition; a complex bulk/portfolio migration can be a
   paid project. No invented prices; structural.

Angle: reframe-a-chore-as-an-asset, name-the-tradeoff (free-to-win vs paid-for-complex), the-failure-
is-invisible (a migration done wrong loses data silently — verify before cutover).
