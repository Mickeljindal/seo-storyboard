## Information gain (the approval question)

The reframe most agencies need: **the server is not the product, the management is.** Agencies leave
money on the table two ways: passing hosting through at cost (billing the commodity, giving away the
value), or bundling it invisibly so the client never sees a line item they would happily pay for.

- Three pricing models named and compared: cost-plus markup, flat tiered plans, and value-based
  (bundled into a care plan). With when each fits.
- Real margin math, with numbers EXPLICITLY labelled as illustrative examples (not Kloudbean prices),
  showing how consolidating many clients onto one managed account changes the unit economics versus
  one account per client.
- What is genuinely billable beyond the raw server: management, monitoring, backups and tested
  restores, updates and patching, an uptime/response commitment, and the security posture. Naming these
  is what justifies a margin that "reselling a VPS" cannot.
- The predictability argument: you cannot safely mark up a bill you cannot predict. A metered,
  surprise-prone underlying cost makes fixed client pricing risky; a predictable base is what lets an
  agency quote a flat monthly price with confidence. (Ties to cloud-hosting-pricing-explained.)
- A founder opinion: charge for hosting as a managed service, or fold it into a care plan, but do not
  pass it through at cost. Passing through at cost trains the client to see hosting as a commodity and
  leaves your real work unpaid.

Angles: the-status-quo-hides-the-real-cause (pass-through-at-cost undercharges the management), name-
the-tradeoff (bundled vs itemised), a real decision cue.

## Accuracy / claims

NO invented Kloudbean prices. Any figure is an illustrative example, labelled as such, to demonstrate
margin mechanics only. Confirmed facts used lightly: one account holds many clients (consolidation),
subusers give a billing-contact role, predictable base pricing supports markup, automatic backups /
patching / free SSL are part of what makes "management" real and billable. Never promise the agency a
specific margin or income. The honest boundary: the platform is the cost base and the toolset; pricing
and client relationships are the agency's.
