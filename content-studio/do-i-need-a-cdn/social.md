It depends, mostly on two things: how spread out your audience is, and how much of your content is static.

A CDN caches static files (images, video, CSS, JavaScript) at edge locations near your users, which cuts latency, takes load off your origin server, and softens traffic spikes. If your audience is global or your site is asset-heavy, it helps a lot. If you serve a small local audience or mostly dynamic, per-user pages, it does very little.
