## Cannibalisation guard (mandatory)

The playbook has "Phase 2: The per-client onboarding checklist" as an OVERVIEW section inside a
12-part strategy page. Risk of overlap is real, so this page is scoped as the **operational runbook**:
the actual repeatable steps, a copy-usable intake list, verification commands, and a handover email
template. It explicitly defers the "why this shape" to the playbook Phase 2 and links there, rather
than restating the strategy. The playbook is the why; this is the doing.

## Information gain

- A phased runbook you can literally follow: Intake, Provision, Build/migrate, Verify, Handover, Bill.
- A "definition of done" checklist so onboarding has an unambiguous finish line (the thing most agencies
  lack, which is why sites go live half-configured).
- A copy-usable client intake list (what to collect BEFORE touching anything, so you are not chasing
  DNS access mid-migration).
- Go-live verification as concrete checks (SSL present and auto-renewing, backups actually running,
  staging exists, monitoring on, scoped client access created) rather than "make sure it works".
- A handover email template, because the client's experience of onboarding is the email, not your work.
- The isolation reminder: a new client is a new walled-off environment, not a folder on a shared box.

Angle: the-fix-is-structural (a definition of done retires the half-configured launch), plus a real
reusable artifact (the intake list + email template).

## Claims (facts files only)

Subusers/UAC (scoped client access), staging (WordPress + Laravel), automatic backups, free SSL
issued and renewed, free migration assistance, cron jobs from the UI, one dashboard, 7 clouds. No
white-label branding claim. No invented prices.

## Format

Runbook. Phases with checklists, one code block of verification commands, one email template in a
pre/code block, a definition-of-done list, common mistakes, honest boundary, FAQ. Deliberately
operational, not strategic.
