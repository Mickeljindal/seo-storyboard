## Information gain

The stance most agencies get wrong under stress: a client leaving still owns their data, and how you
offboard is a reference you are writing for yourself. The article is a clean-exit runbook:
- Export everything in portable form (files, database, media) with real commands.
- Transfer: hand to the client, or to the new agency, including the DNS cutover away from you.
- Revoke access as a deliberate step (the revocation drill), not an afterthought that leaves a former
  client or contractor with a live login for months.
- Final invoice and stop the recurring charge, so you are not billing someone who left or, worse,
  giving free hosting by forgetting.
- Retention then deletion: keep a backup for the agreed window, then actually delete, because holding a
  former client's data forever is a liability, not a courtesy.
- The anti-pattern named plainly: holding data hostage to force a last payment. It is the fastest way
  to a bad review and sometimes a legal problem; separate the invoice from the handover.

Angle: concede-and-do-the-right-thing (you lost the client; win the reference), name-the-tradeoff
(retention window vs liability), a reusable artifact (export commands + email).
